# Voice Analysis Framework

Work through all 7 dimensions below. For each, pull **2–4 verbatim examples** from the source material. If a dimension is unclear from what's available, note it and flag low confidence.

## Dimension 1 — Voice Signature

The irreducible personality of the voice. Synthesize:

- **Core personality**: Distill to 2–3 adjectives + brief explanation. (e.g., "Direct but warm — makes confident claims without sounding cold")
- **Energy**: Does the prose move fast or slowly? Does it accelerate or stay steady?
- **Emotional register**: Warm/cool? Passionate/measured? Confident/exploratory?
- **Reader feeling**: What should a reader feel after finishing a piece? (e.g., "Like they just had a sharp, honest conversation with a smart friend")

---

## Dimension 2 — Sentence Architecture

The physical structure of writing at the sentence and paragraph level:

- **Sentence length**: What's the dominant pattern? Short (<10 words)? Medium (10–25)? Long (25+)? Mixed deliberately?
- **Fragments**: Does the writer use intentional fragments for emphasis? How often?
- **Rhythm**: Do sentence lengths vary rhythmically? (e.g., long-long-short as a punctuation move)
- **Paragraph length**: Dense blocks? Short punchy paragraphs? One-sentence paragraphs for emphasis?
- **Run-ons**: Any deliberate breathless constructions?

---

## Dimension 3 — Lexical Fingerprint

The specific vocabulary that makes this voice recognizable:

- **Power words**: Words they reach for repeatedly that carry weight or signal confidence (e.g., "exactly", "actually", "the real question is")
- **Characteristic phrases**: Multi-word expressions that are distinctly theirs (e.g., "the thing people miss", "here's what I mean by that")
- **Transition style**: How do they move between ideas? (e.g., "Which means...", "But here's the thing —", "Let me be specific.")
- **Avoided vocabulary**: What formal, jargon-y, or AI-flavored words do they never use? Specifically check for: *delve, leverage, utilize, it's worth noting, underscore, ensure, streamline, robust, empower, in today's fast-paced world, let's explore* — note which are absent
- **Contractions**: Do they use them? How frequently? (signals formality register)
- **Technical vocabulary**: Domain-specific terms they use naturally without over-explaining
- **Colloquialisms**: Casual phrasing, idioms, informal constructions
- **Intensifiers/qualifiers**: How do they add emphasis or nuance? (really, genuinely, kind of, a bit, quite, deeply)

---

## Dimension 4 — Structural Patterns

How the writer constructs a complete piece:

- **Openings**: What's the dominant pattern? Question? Bold claim? Concrete scene? Contrast/tension? Surprising data? Personal anecdote? Pull 2–3 example opening lines.
- **Argument architecture**: How do they build to a point?
  - Conclusion-first (thesis → support)?
  - Build-up to reveal (setup → payoff)?
  - Spiral (return to an idea from multiple angles)?
  - Narrative (story → lesson)?
- **Closings**: How do they land a piece? Call to action? Reflection? Loop back to the opener? Open question?
- **List usage**: Do they use bullet lists? How long? Or do they write lists in prose ("three things: X, Y, and Z")?
- **Example style**: Principle first, then example? Or concrete first, then abstraction?
- **White space and pacing**: Do they use short paragraphs or questions to create breathing room?

---

## Dimension 5 — Rhetorical Moves

The writer's toolkit for engaging the reader:

- **Questions**: Rhetorical (answer already implied) or genuinely open? How often?
- **Self-disclosure**: How personal do they get? Do they share failures, opinions, personal stakes? What's the vulnerability threshold?
- **Uncertainty and hedging**: Do they hedge ("I think", "maybe", "probably") or make bold claims? Do they acknowledge counterarguments?
- **Disagreement style**: How do they push back? Blunt? Diplomatic? Ironic?
- **Humor**: Dry, self-deprecating, absurdist, or none? How frequent? Embedded in sentences or set-piece?
- **Storytelling**: First-person experiences? Third-person examples? Hypotheticals? How long?
- **Direct address**: Do they speak directly to "you"? How often and in what contexts?

---

## Dimension 6 — What This Voice Is NOT

As important as everything above. Explicitly catalog:

- **Never-used constructions**: Phrasings or rhetorical devices that appear zero times and would feel alien
- **Off-register tones**: What would immediately read as "not them" — overly corporate, academic, casual, etc.
- **AI writing red flags**: Common AI patterns that DO NOT appear in the source. Check specifically: *"delve into", "it's worth noting that", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's explore", "I'd be happy to", listicle format with bold headers for each point, excessive hedging on every sentence*
- **Structural taboos**: Things they never do (e.g., never uses bullet lists, never ends with a question, never starts with "I")

---

## Dimension 7 — Signature Examples

Pull **4–6 verbatim passages** that are quintessentially this person — sentences or short paragraphs a close reader would immediately say "yes, that's them."

For each, add a label explaining what makes it representative:

> *"[verbatim quote]"*
> — **[Label]**: e.g., "Classic opening move — tension before context", "Shows the long-short rhythm pattern", "How they signal disagreement without being combative"

---

## Source Type Weighting

Weight transcript vs. written material differently:

- **Transcripts** reveal authentic rhythm, vocabulary under pressure, and how the person naturally structures ideas when not editing themselves. Trust these for: power words, pacing, rhetorical moves, hedging patterns.
- **Written content** captures intentional craft — openings and closings they've chosen to keep, vocabulary selected during editing. Trust these for: structural patterns, argument architecture, opening/closing style.

When both are available, note in the analysis which dimensions drew primarily from which source type. When only transcripts are available, flag that Dimensions 4 (structural patterns) and the closing style may shift once the person writes vs. speaks.

# Hook Discovery Prompts

Use when a builder is stuck — they have a fuzzy idea but no hook. Pick two or three of these, not all. The goal is to surface, not to interrogate.

## Memory prompts

- What's an app or tool you've used where you remember a specific *moment* — not what it does, but a moment? What was happening? What about the design made that moment feel different?
- What's an app you downloaded and deleted within a week? What specifically lost you? (The answer often points at what their app must do *not* to fail the same way.)
- When you imagine someone using your app, what is the moment you'd want to watch over their shoulder?
- What's something you do manually — in a spreadsheet, a notes app, a group chat — that you've thought "there should be an app for this"? What's actually stopping that app from existing?

## Verb prompts

- If your app were a single verb, what would it be? (Track, connect, remind, simplify, decide, find, block, automate.) If two verbs feel equally right, you may have two products.
- What do you *not* want the verb to be? (Equally informative — rules out a lot of design space and often surfaces a strong opinion about what the app isn't.)

## Constraint prompts

- If you could only keep one of: solves the problem offline, works in under 30 seconds, requires no learning curve, handles edge cases perfectly — which is non-negotiable for your user? (Forces a priority that shapes every design decision.)
- What's a rule you *won't* break for this product? (No dark patterns? No ads? Never asks for more permissions than it needs? No account required?) Constraints make the product.

## Negative-space prompts

- What existing app does yours sit *next to* — and what does yours do that it doesn't? If you can't answer this clearly, you may not have a real gap yet.
- Who would *not* want this app, and is that okay? A product that is perfect for one group and wrong for another is usually stronger than something that "works for everyone."
- If a competitor launched this exact product tomorrow, what would you say it's missing? That's often the core of what yours needs to be.

## User-moment prompts

- Describe the single worst moment your target user has with the problem your app solves. What are they doing, where are they, what just went wrong? Your app exists to prevent or fix that moment.
- What does the user do *instead* right now? Is their workaround so good that your app has to be significantly better — or is their workaround so bad that anything better wins?
- After using your app, what does the user tell a friend? "I found this app that..." — finish the sentence. If you can't finish it, the value proposition isn't clear yet.

## The one-sentence test

End any ideation session by asking the builder to fill in this template *out loud*:

> "It's an app that helps [specific user] do [specific thing] when [specific context], and the key difference from what they're using now is [specific differentiator]."

If they can fill it in cleanly, the concept is solid. If they can't fill in any one of the four blanks, that's where the work is:

- Can't name the user → the audience is too broad; find the one person who desperately needs this
- Can't name the thing → the value proposition is still fuzzy; keep asking "what specifically does it do?"
- Can't name the context → the use case isn't real yet; watch someone have the problem
- Can't name the differentiator → the gap isn't found yet; spend more time in the App Store

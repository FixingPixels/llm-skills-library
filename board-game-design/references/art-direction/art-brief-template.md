# Art Brief Template

Use one brief per commissioned illustration. Reuse this structure for cover art, faction key art, character portraits, card illustrations, and major board pieces.

## Brief structure

```
PROJECT: [Game Name]
PIECE: [What this is — Box Cover, Faction A Key Art, Card Illustration #14: "Last Light", etc.]
ARTIST: [Name]
DUE: [Date]
USE / DIMENSIONS: [Where this will appear and at what size]
BUDGET: [Agreed fee]

----

PURPOSE
[1-3 sentences. What this image needs to do for the game. Sell the box / show this faction / illustrate this card.]

WORLD CONTEXT
[1-2 paragraphs. The world the image lives in — when, where, what's happening. Tell the artist what they need to know, not the whole world bible.]

SCENE DESCRIPTION
[The image itself. Who or what is in it; what they're doing; what the moment is. Be specific about *the moment* — the second the image captures.]

EXAMPLE: "A sentinel stands at the gate at dusk. Lantern just lit. Wind in their cloak. They've heard something but don't yet know what. Behind them, a sliver of warm light from the open gate; ahead, fog and the suggestion of movement."

MOOD & TONE
[Two or three adjectives. "Quiet, foreboding, beautiful." "Triumphant, warm, mythic." Avoid "epic" — meaningless.]

STYLE REFERENCES
[At least three reference images, each tagged with what to take from it.]

- [Image 1 link or name]: take the lighting, especially the warm-cool contrast.
- [Image 2 link or name]: take the composition — single figure, off-center, large negative space.
- [Image 3 link or name]: take the brushwork — visible, painterly, not photoreal.

PALETTE GUIDANCE
[Either a color study, named palette colors with hex, or descriptive: "muted earth tones with one cold accent (ice blue) on the figure's lantern flame."]

DON'T
[Anti-references — what *not* to do. Critical for staying out of common pitfalls.]

- Don't render anything photorealistically.
- Don't lean into high fantasy clichés (no horns on the helmet, no glowing runes).
- Don't put the focal point dead center — the title will sit there.

DELIVERABLES
- [Thumbnail / composition sketch] — by [date]
- [Color study] — by [date]
- [Final at full resolution] — by [date]
- [Source file format expected — .psd layered, etc.]

LICENSE
[How will the work be used — print, digital, marketing, expansion uses, exclusivity. State plainly. Match what's in the contract.]

CREDIT
[How the artist will be credited — on the box, on the rulebook, on social. Standard is full credit visible.]
```

## What separates a strong brief from a weak one

**Weak**:
> Need fantasy art for a cover. A warrior in front of a castle, dramatic, epic.

**Strong**:
> Need cover art for a 90-minute strategic euro about civic rebuilding after a war. Audience: hobby gamers, weight 3.5/5, similar to *Brass: Birmingham* and *Nucleum* aesthetically. Image: a single figure (a builder or stonemason, gender-ambiguous) silhouetted against a half-rebuilt cathedral at dawn. Their hand is on the stone. The light is warm but tired. We don't see their face. 3:4 aspect, 2400×3200 at 300dpi for box use.
>
> Mood: hopeful but exhausted. Something has been won, but at cost.
>
> References: composition like [reference 1], lighting like [reference 2], brushwork like [reference 3].
>
> Don't: render photorealistically; don't show weapons; don't include flying creatures or magical effects.
>
> Title and player count will overlay the top third — keep that area uncluttered.

The difference is specificity, anti-references, and naming the moment.

## When the artist comes back with the wrong piece

Sometimes the first pass misses. Diagnose before redirecting:

- Did your **brief** describe the wrong thing? Often the brief said "warrior at castle" when you meant "the moment after the battle."
- Did the artist **misread** the brief? Look at where they diverged — that's a clue about what was unclear.
- Did the artist follow the brief but the brief was **wrong about the project**? You may have been wrong about what the cover needed.

Don't ask for full repaints if you can help it. Smaller asks ("can the figure face the other way?" or "warmer light from the right") cost the artist less and stay within scope.

## Brief volume and pacing

A 50-card game commissioned individually will have 50 briefs. That's overwhelming. Strategies:

- **Tier the work.** Box cover gets a full brief. Faction key art gets a full brief. Card illustrations might get a 5-line brief each.
- **Brief the artist on the world once**, then per-piece briefs assume that context.
- **Group similar pieces.** Six "wilderness" cards can share a style brief; six "city" cards another.
- **Iterate in batches.** Get 5 thumbnails before any rendering, so you can correct course early.

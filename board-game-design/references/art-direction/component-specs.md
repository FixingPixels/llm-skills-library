# Component Specs Checklist

Before manufacturing — even before commissioning final art — every physical component should have a spec. Use this as a checklist; fill in for your specific project.

## Box

- **Dimensions** (W × D × H, in mm or inches)
- **Box-bottom depth** (relevant for inserts)
- **Material** — chipboard standard, premium options (linen-wrap, foil)
- **Finish** — matte, gloss, soft-touch, spot UV on title
- **Print** — full-color outside; inside often blank or solid color
- **Insert / organizer** — none, plastic insert, custom wooden insert, designed cardboard divisions

## Cards

- **Dimensions** — common: poker (63.5×88mm), bridge (57×88mm), tarot (70×120mm), mini (45×68mm), large square (88×88mm). Pick *one* size per deck.
- **Card stock weight** — 280gsm standard, 310gsm premium, 350gsm+ for heavy use cards
- **Finish** — linen, smooth, matte, gloss, plastic-coated. Linen is the hobby standard for hand-shuffled cards.
- **Bleed** — standard 3mm bleed; account for in art files
- **Front/back design** — same back across the deck (so backs don't telegraph what the card is)
- **Card count** — total per deck; in groups of 18 or 54 are most cost-efficient at most printers

## Board

- **Dimensions unfolded** (W × H)
- **Fold pattern** — bi-fold, quad-fold, six-fold, hex-fold; affects how the board lies flat
- **Material weight** — standard 2mm chipboard mounted; premium options heavier
- **Finish** — matte preferred; gloss reflects light at table
- **Print** — full-color one side; back usually blank or single color
- **Layout zones** — define every functional zone on the board with a grid (action spaces, score track, market, etc.)

## Tokens, cubes, meeples

- **Material** — wood (organic, premium feel, $$), plastic (precise shapes, $$), cardboard punch-out (cheapest, durable enough for most uses)
- **Shape** — generic cube vs. custom shaped meeple; custom shapes cost more but communicate more
- **Color** — standard player colors (red, blue, yellow, green, white, black, orange, purple)
- **Quantity per player and total** — over-include by ~10% to allow for losses
- **Engraving / printing** — wooden tokens can be screen-printed or engraved; engraving is premium

## Dice

- **Type and quantity** — d6 standard; custom faces (custom-printed) increase cost meaningfully
- **Material** — plastic standard, wooden d6 premium-feel, metal premium-luxe
- **Size** — 12mm small, 16mm standard, 19mm+ for spectacle
- **Color** — match player colors or use neutral
- **Faces** — pips vs. numbers vs. custom symbols; custom symbols require manufacturing setup fee

## Miniatures

- **Material** — plastic injection (best for high volumes, expensive setup), resin (lower volume, higher cost per unit), pre-painted plastic (premium, expensive)
- **Scale** — 28mm, 32mm, 54mm common for tabletop scale
- **Sculpting** — usually requires a specialist; budget can be $200-$2000 per sculpt
- **Painting** — most miniatures ship unpainted; pre-painted is a premium feature
- **Stand / base** — included with sculpt or separate?

## Insert / organizer

- **Function** — does it organize cards by deck? Hold tokens? Allow setup-without-resorting?
- **Material** — plastic vacuum-formed, custom wooden, custom cardboard insert
- **Punch-out cardboard** — many indie publishers use designed cardboard inserts that ship flat and assemble; cheap and effective
- **Compatibility with sleeved cards** — important if your audience sleeves; plan for thicker decks

## Player boards / aids

- **Dimensions and fold** — single-sheet vs. fold-out; size in mm
- **Material** — cardboard (standard), heavier card stock (player aid sized)
- **Front/back** — often dual-purpose (Side A: standard mode; Side B: solo or advanced)
- **Per-player count** — usually one per player

## Rulebook / scenario book

- **Page count** — multiple of 4 (often 8, 12, 16, 24 pages)
- **Size** — match the box footprint or be slightly smaller
- **Paper weight** — 100-130gsm common; heavier for premium feel
- **Binding** — saddle-stitch (staple, common up to 32 pages), perfect-bound (glued spine, for thicker books)
- **Cover** — same paper or heavier card; matte vs. gloss
- **Print** — full-color throughout

## Tuckbox or similar (for card-only games)

- **Dimensions** — match card size + sleeve depth
- **Material** — chipboard with full-color print; premium options foiled
- **Finish** — matte common
- **Closure** — tuck flap standard

## Currency considerations

- **Manufacturing minimums** — most overseas printers require 1500-3000 unit minimums. Below that, look at print-on-demand (Game Crafter, Drive Thru Cards) or domestic small-run printers.
- **Printer choice affects everything** — different printers have different stocks, sizes, and finish options. Talk to your printer *before* finalizing specs.
- **Setup fees vs. unit costs** — high-setup options (custom dice, custom miniatures, foil printing) make sense for high-volume runs but kill economics on small runs.
- **Prototype vs. final** — print-on-demand for prototype units; switch to volume printing for final production.

## Common spec mistakes

- **Mismatched card sizes** — if any deck is a different size, players will fumble. Consolidate.
- **Box too big for contents** — looks underfilled; insert + filler can fix.
- **Box too small for contents** — sleeved cards don't fit. Test with the audience that will sleeve.
- **Iconography too small for the print scale** — designed at screen-size, falls apart at production print size.
- **Bleed not accounted for** — art runs to the edge and gets clipped. Always design with bleed.
- **Color drift between proof and final** — order a print proof from the actual production printer before approving.
- **Insert designed before final component count** — components keep changing in development; don't lock the insert spec until late.

## Talking to printers

A good printer is a partner. Solo hobbyists benefit from talking to printers early, even before final specs. Common partners for indie publishers:

- **The Game Crafter** — print-on-demand, low minimums, good for prototypes and small runs
- **Whatz Games / Panda / Longpack / Wingo / LongPack / Boda / Daly / etc.** — full-service overseas printers, larger minimums
- **DriveThruCards** — print-on-demand for cards specifically
- **MakePlayingCards** — solid mid-range card printing

Always get test prints before committing to a full run. Costs $30-$100; saves thousands.

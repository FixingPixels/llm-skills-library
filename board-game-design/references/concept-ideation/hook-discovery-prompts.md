# Hook Discovery Prompts

Use when a designer is stuck — they have a fuzzy idea but no hook. Pick two or three of these, not all. The goal is to surface, not to interrogate.

## Memory prompts

- What's a board game you played where you remember a specific *moment* — not who won, but a moment? What was happening? What about the design made that moment possible?
- What's a game you bounced off, and what specifically lost you? (The answer often points at what their game must do *not* to fail the same way.)
- When you imagine someone playing your game, what is the moment you'd want to watch over their shoulder?

## Verb prompts

- If your game were a single verb, what would it be? (Build, hide, betray, race, deduce, gather, defend, accuse.) If two verbs feel equally right, you may have two games.
- What do you *not* want the verb to be? (Equally informative — rules out a lot of design space.)

## Constraint prompts

- If you could only ship one of: deep strategy, fast play, beautiful components, vivid theme — which is non-negotiable? (Forces a priority.)
- What's a rule you *won't* break for this game? (Length under an hour? No score-tracking? No reading? Solo-only? Constraints make the game.)

## Negative-space prompts

- What existing game does yours sit *next to* — and what does yours do that it doesn't?
- What kind of player are you specifically designing for? Who would *not* like this game, and is that okay?
- If a publisher asked "why does this exist when [closest competitor] already does," what's your answer?

## Fantasy prompts

- What does the player get to *be* in this game that they can't be elsewhere?
- What's a scene from a movie, book, or memory that you'd want to recreate at the table?
- If a player put down the box and called a friend after, what would they say happened?

## The one-sentence test

End any ideation session by asking the user to fill in this template *out loud*:

> "It's a ___-player game where you ___ in order to ___, and the twist is ___."

If they can fill it in cleanly, the concept is solid. If they can't fill in any one of the four blanks, that's where the work is.

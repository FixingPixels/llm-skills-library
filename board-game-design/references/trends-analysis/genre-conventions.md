# Genre Conventions

Quick reference of what audiences expect from common board game genres. Use to flag where a designer is meeting expectations, breaking them deliberately, or breaking them by accident.

This is grounded in training-knowledge state of the hobby. Specific releases beyond training cutoff need live research.

## Eurogames / strategy

**Audience expects**: Tight resource economy, indirect interaction, multiple paths to victory, low randomness, calculable optimal play, 60-120 minute length, painterly art, end-game scoring categories, low-luck end states.

**Common verbs**: Place, build, convert, draft, score.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Multiplayer solitaire is a frequent complaint. Engine games can have boring middles. End-game scoring tally can drag.

**Where the audience is changing**: Cozier themes (*Wingspan*, *Cascadia*), more solo modes, lighter touches (gateway euros like *Splendor* and *Azul* opening the audience).

## Ameritrash / thematic

**Audience expects**: Direct conflict, dice/cards as randomizers, narrative arcs, miniatures, vivid theme, dramatic moments, asymmetric powers, swingy outcomes that produce stories.

**Common verbs**: Attack, explore, defend, betray, quest.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Length creep (4+ hour epics losing audience patience). Player elimination (fewer modern designs do it). Imbalance excused by "thematic" — audiences are less forgiving than they used to be.

**Where the audience is changing**: Hybridization with euro elements (the "Amerithrash" middle ground). Co-op campaign arcs (*Gloomhaven*, *Sleeping Gods*) replacing PvP epics for many groups.

## Cooperative

**Audience expects**: Shared goals, scaling difficulty, an "AI" or system that pushes back, win/lose conditions that feel earned, low alpha-player problem (designs that prevent one player from dictating).

**Common verbs**: Coordinate, defend, deliver, escape.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Alpha-player / quarterbacking. Difficulty curves that frustrate (too hard for newcomers, too easy for veterans). "Solved" co-ops where the optimal play is mechanical.

**Where the audience is changing**: Heavier co-ops are accepted (*Spirit Island*, *Frosthaven*). Solo-as-co-op is normal. Hidden info / hidden role hybrids gaining traction.

## Party / family

**Audience expects**: Fast play (15-30 min), low complexity, high player count tolerance (4-8), accessibility for non-gamers, social interaction, low barrier to teach.

**Common verbs**: Guess, perform, draw, vote, call.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Designs that *think* they're party games but require strategy or learning — the audience will reject them at parties.

**Where the audience is changing**: Word/deduction party games strong (*Just One*, *Decrypto*). Party games extending into the 6-10 player range. Cooperative party games (*Magic Maze*) carving out a niche.

## Dexterity

**Audience expects**: Physical skill matters, components are the toy, short rules, social spectacle.

**Common verbs**: Flick, stack, balance, drop.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Storage and table-space problems. Components prone to damage.

**Where the audience is changing**: A small but devoted resurgence (*Flick 'em Up*, *Junk Art*, *Catacombs*). Hybrid dexterity-strategy designs occasionally land.

## Roll-and-write / flip-and-write

**Audience expects**: Small footprint, fast play (20-40 min), high player count tolerance, replay through different randomness, satisfying optimization within constraints.

**Common verbs**: Mark, write, plan, draft.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Designs that should have been roll-and-writes but added a board (footprint creep). Designs that should have been bigger but were forced into the small format.

**Where the audience is changing**: Flip-and-write (cards instead of dice) gaining over roll-and-write. Solo-friendly designs nearly default. Some moves toward "write-and-pass" interactions.

## Trick-taking

**Audience expects**: Hand management, reading the table, partnership or solo, fast turns, classic feel.

**Common verbs**: Lead, follow, trump, take.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Designs that bolt unrelated systems onto a trick-taking base — usually fails. Trick-takers that don't reward reading other players.

**Where the audience is changing**: *The Crew* showed appetite for trick-takers with novel hooks (cooperative, communication-limited). Niche but committed.

## 18xx / heavy economic

**Audience expects**: Long play (3-6 hours), open information, train-and-share market, intense player interaction, mathematical depth, no apologies for complexity.

**Common verbs**: Buy, sell, pay, dump.

**Pitfalls to flag**: New designers wading in without playing 5+ existing 18xx games first. The audience is small and demanding.

**Where the audience is changing**: Slow but steady. Crowdfunding has supported titles a publisher wouldn't have. Audience is aging; some efforts to make on-ramps (*1830* still the canonical entry).

## Wargames

**Audience expects**: Historical/simulation accuracy, hex-and-counter or card-driven, asymmetric scenarios, long play, dense rulebooks, dedicated audience.

**Common verbs**: Move, attack, supply, retreat.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Audience is small, technical, and unforgiving of factual or scale errors. New designers should engage with the existing community before designing.

**Where the audience is changing**: Card-driven wargames have widened the audience. *COIN* series, *Twilight Struggle*, *Root* (war-game-influenced) opened bridges. The traditional hex-and-counter audience is steady but aging.

## Dungeon crawl / RPG-adjacent

**Audience expects**: Character progression, asymmetric heroes, scenario-driven play, narrative scenes, miniatures, campaign or legacy arcs, replayability through choices.

**Common verbs**: Explore, fight, level up, choose.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Long teach times. Often cumbersome setup. Miniature painting expectations adding to perceived cost. Saturated by Kickstarter epics.

**Where the audience is changing**: *Gloomhaven* expanded the audience hugely. *Sleeping Gods* and similar narrative-first designs winning over pure-combat dungeon crawls. Solo-first designs gaining ground.

## Cozy / aesthetic-first

**Audience expects**: Beautiful components and art, low conflict, satisfying engines, soft themes (nature, cooking, gardening, exploration), 30-60 min play, accessible weight.

**Common verbs**: Place, gather, observe, arrange.

**Pitfalls to flag**: Many cozy games are *very* similar mechanically. Differentiation often lives in theme and art rather than mechanics.

**Where the audience is changing**: Strong, growing, broadening into solo-friendly and family-weight designs. Audience is more diverse than traditional hobby gamers — designers should not assume traditional hobby conventions translate.
